Methodological Architecture

The Empirical Protocol, Step by Step

How data was harvested, filtered, categorized, and quantified; precisely what it represents; and, with identical scientific vigilance, what conclusions one cannot validly extrapolate. I delineate every phase sequentially. An empirical finding is only as robust as the methodology that produced it, and omitting the methodology would be, frankly, an intolerable breach of scientific etiquette.

Notice: This represents a structured sample, not an exhaustive census. The tally of videos and posts does not represent the entirety of items existing across social platforms. Rather, they comprise records returned by algorithmic search queries within defined pagination thresholds, subsequently subjected to strict thematic filtering. Commercial social platforms do not expose universal archives, and search APIs inherently favor recent and viral material. Consequently, annual volume reflects sample capture rather than absolute population size. Valid scientific comparisons are strictly confined to proportions within the harvested sample (thematic distribution, sentiment posture, inquiry archetypes) and direct engagement telemetry of collected items (views, likes, comments).

Quantification Protocol: Reach, Likes, and Comments

Each platform exposes divergent telemetry schemas. An unyielding rule governing our analysis: never aggregate physically disparate dimensions. Video views and follower counts are strictly non-additive scalars.

Reach (Audience Exposure)

Video views officially reported by YouTube and TikTok on harvest date. These represent cumulative snapshots: older publications naturally accumulate larger exposure over time. Instagram restricts view telemetry on harvested image/carousel pages; there we calculate potential audience (aggregate follower counts of profiles posting on-topic). A follower is unequivocally not a view; hence, these metrics are never conflated or summed.

Likes and Comments

On YouTube and TikTok, these reflect platform-reported totals. On Instagram, likes displayed on the post (some accounts elect to suppress public like counts) and comments successfully collected via scraping, which may represent a subset of platform totals. On X (formerly Twitter), API/scraper returns yielded zero values across likes, replies, and reposts for all 427 harvested tweets—a manifest interface parsing anomaly, as the probability of 427 on-topic tweets yielding exactly zero interactions is statistically infinitesimal. Consequently, X is represented strictly in publication volume and collected reply threads.

Thematic Filtration Protocol

A subset of data harvesting was executed at profile scope (retrieving all recent publications of identified creators), inevitably introducing off-topic material. Consequently, an Instagram, TikTok, or X publication is admitted into the corpus if and only if caption text, post copy, or Whisper-transcribed audio contains at least one of the following canonical tokens: superdotação, superdotado, altas habilidades, AH/SD, QI, IQ, Mensa, gifted, superinteligente, gênio, prodígio, neurodivergência, dupla excepcionalidade, or inteligência. YouTube videos previously underwent rigorous manual decontamination to eliminate homonym artifacts (e.g., Ghanaian pastor Mensa Otabil and American hip-hop artist Vic Mensa).

Every algorithmic filter entails classification trade-offs, operating along a precision-recall Pareto frontier. Ours is moderately permissive, as terms such as "inteligência" and "gênio" occasionally admit tangential philosophical discussions; simultaneously, it is moderately restrictive, excluding creators discussing twice-exceptionality without explicitly deploying defined keywords.

Natural Language Classification Architecture

Our natural language system operates strictly as a discrete classification pipeline. It receives normalized text, evaluates semantic embeddings against an invariant rubric, and assigns a single category from a predefined closed schema while computing a confidence probability score. It generates no conversational prose, injects no editorial commentary, and invents no ad-hoc classes.

Thematic Taxonomy

  • 13 standardized themes applied uniformly across public user comments, produced videos, and press reports.
  • All 80,809 harvested YouTube comments evaluated: 65,265 classified via NLP and 15,544 devoid of lexical tokens (pure emoji strings, user mentions) assigned to "other".
  • 353 videos (evaluated across title, channel metadata, and description) and 1,264 news reports (title and full body text).
  • Semantic boundary definitions for each topic were grounded in preliminary empirical samples and documented in the project codebook.

Sentiment Posture Toward Mensa

  • Evaluated strictly for publications explicitly citing Mensa by name.
  • 1,461 user comments, 143 videos, and 408 news reports.
  • Ternary classification schema: supporter, neutral, or critic/detractor.

Inquiries and Psychological Distress

  • Candidate pool: comments from 2021 to 2026 formulating questions or deploying vocabulary of psychological distress (5,248 on YouTube, 1,499 on Instagram).
  • Stratified sample of 1,000 YouTube and 500 Instagram comments: 50% highest engagement, 50% randomized control.
  • 10 clinical/existential categories plus "none"; 1,498 successfully processed, 605 confirmed genuine dilemmas.
  • Exemplars displayed on the site require a model classification confidence score ≥ 0.70.

Model Validation Status

No automated classification has yet been double-blind benchmarked against human inter-rater reliability. A stratified sample of 275 items is currently structured for subsequent inter-coder validation. In the interim, percentage values reflect model classification distributions rather than certified ground truth. This distinction is scientifically critical, and we prefer absolute transparency over pretending to possess a precision metric not yet experimentally established.

Lexical Mining, Creator Clustering, and Institutional Screening

Lexical Domains

11 curated token dictionaries (Mensa; IQ & Psychometrics; ADHD, Autism & Mental Health; Clinical Assessment; Schooling & Education; Legislation & Policy; Childhood & Family; Humor & Trivia; Genius & Prodigies; Elitism; Adult Identification). A publication intersects a domain if it contains at least one dictionary token, establishing an identical measurement scale across platforms, outlets, and creators.

Unsupervised Creator Clustering

Creators with ≥ 3 on-topic publications. For each profile, we compute the multi-dimensional vector representing the proportion of content intersecting each lexical domain. Applied K-Means clustering (k=6) with 25 random restarts. Cluster nomenclature reflects principal statistical centroids distinguishing each cohort from universe mean.

Prospective Institutional Partners

Creators with ≥ 3 on-topic publications and ≥ 50,000 reach (video views or followers). Quintile scores (1 to 5) across three objective criteria: reach magnitude; editorial affinity (proportion of substantive clinical/school/family/policy content minus 50% trivia weight); and conversation depth (comments per post).

Adjacent Word Pairs (Bigrams)

Adjacent token pairs co-occurring across documents, deduplicated per document. Purged of syntactic stop words, numerals (quiz answers), tokens shorter than three characters, and platform interface artifacts. Displays the top 20 most frequent adjacent pairs per facet appearing across at least 3 distinct documents.

User Privacy and Data Sanitization

Author identities for public YouTube comments were irreversibly pseudonymized via salted one-way SHA-256 cryptographic hashes and do not appear anywhere in this observatory. No personal comment is attributed to an identifiable author, and strings containing phone numbers, URLs, or personal tags were purged. Exclusively verified public profiles of creators, media outlets, and registered institutions appear by name. The key bridging pseudonymized tokens to original identifiers is strictly air-gapped on restricted local storage.
